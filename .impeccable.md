# Impeccable

<!-- impeccable:design-context 1 -->

Scope: the TV frontend in `frontend/` (Titan Browse). The Python backend in
`src/tv_avatar/` has no UI and is out of scope. Source documents:
`frontend/PRODUCT.md` (users, constraints, product principles) and
`frontend/design.md` (the incumbent visual system and the rationale behind it).

## Design Context

### Users

Viewers in a European household, ~10 feet (3 m) from a television, holding a remote.
No pointer, no keyboard — four arrows, OK, Back, four colour keys, transport keys.
One set is shared: up to five profiles, each adult or kids, browsing the same catalogue
from the same sofa and needing different results from it.

The job, in the viewer's words: *find something worth watching in the next few seconds,
or get back into what I was already watching.* Nobody sits down to operate a browse
screen. Browsing is the tax on watching; the product's job is to keep it small.

The hardware is the user's context as much as the sofa is: Titan OS sets from 2020–2022,
Chrome 84, 1–1.5 GB of memory shared with the whole TV OS, a weak GPU, 720p or 1080p and
always 16:9. Success is measured on that board, not in a mock.

### Brand Personality

**Calm · cinematic · effortless.**

The room recedes and the content leads. Sitting down should feel restful; choosing should
feel quick. Nothing in the interface asks to be admired — the expensive-feeling parts are
there so the viewer stops noticing the interface at all.

Voice: plain, specific, written as speech. Errors are addressed to the viewer, never the
developer (*"That file has no title column. Add a column named 'title' and try again."*).
A placeholder says plainly that it is one rather than simulating depth the product does
not have.

### Aesthetic Direction

Dark only. A television in a living room is a light source in a dim space; there is no
light mode and no theme toggle beyond the sky.

**The look is committed, not provisional.** The dark smoked-glass room over a seasonal
sky is the identity now, and future work extends it rather than replacing it. (This
supersedes "Brand Commitments: none committed" in `frontend/PRODUCT.md` and the white-glass
palette still documented in `frontend/design.md` §4.2 and §5 — both predate the current
`:root` tokens in `frontend/src/styles.css` and should be corrected when next touched.)

The system, as it actually stands in code:

- **The room.** A 135° four-stop sky per season (`blossom`, `blue`, `golden`, `ink`),
  optionally a 4K video loop, with pre-rasterised radial lights over it and a scrim graded
  per sky. Never a live `backdrop-filter` — it is patchy in Chrome 84 and ruinous where it
  works.
- **The glass.** Semi-transparent smoke mixed from the sky's own deepest stop
  (`--glass: rgba(7,8,11,.6)`), never a neutral grey, with a 1.5 px light edge.
- **Type.** Schibsted Grotesk 400/600/700/800 with system fallbacks, on a seven-step scale
  at 10-foot sizes: 72 / 56 / 40 / 32 / 24 / 20 / 17 px. Nothing below 17 px.
- **Colour carries meaning, not decoration.** Hierarchy is value — `--ink` / `--ink-2` /
  `--ink-3`, `--primary` / `--chip` / `--chip-quiet`. Only four hues mean anything:
  `--key-red` (My List, matching the physical red key), `--key-yellow` (import, the yellow
  key), `--kids` (a kids profile), `--alert` (consequence, or an action that did not happen).
- **Focus is the loudest state on screen.** Buttons invert to solid white; images take a
  5 px white ring (`--ring`); the selected poster is physically larger (262×334 vs 214×272)
  so selection reads from across a room and in peripheral vision.
- **Spacing** on a 4px base with deliberately far-apart steps, named for the relationship
  each expresses: `--frame` 64, `--gutter` 40, `--pad-panel` 40, `--pad-text` 56,
  `--gap-section` 32, `--gap-block` 24, `--gap-group` 16, `--gap-item` 12, `--gap-tight` 8.
  At 10 feet a 4 px difference is below what the eye resolves, so two intervals closer
  together than one step read as the same interval.
- **Radii** large and consistent: 48 px panels, 24–30 px cards, full pills on buttons.
- **Motion** on `cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1)`: 320 ms row glide, 700 ms player expansion,
  250 ms toasts. One exception: the room folding open or flat runs 1200 ms on a symmetric
  curve (`--ease-room`), because the ease-out is front-loaded enough at that distance to
  read as a snap. Easing is disabled during free scroll, because easing a continuous scroll
  feels like lag. All of it respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.

**Anti-references — what this must not look like:**

- **The Netflix / Disney+ dark-flat grid.** Edge-to-edge posters on flat near-black, no
  room, no depth. Depth here comes from the sky and the glass over it, not from shadows
  faked onto a black plane.
- **A smart-TV OEM launcher.** Titan, Samsung and LG defaults: chunky tiles, mixed icon
  styles, ad rails. The product is a browse layer with a point of view, not a shelf.
- **A glassmorphism trend deck.** Heavy blurs, neon gradients and floating cards for their
  own sake. The glass here exists because it lets one room sit behind every panel for
  almost no per-frame cost — it is a performance strategy that happens to look good, and it
  is drawn without a single blur.

### Design Principles

1. **The remote is the only input.** Every capability is reachable and announceable with
   four arrows, OK and Back. A control the remote cannot reach does not exist. In practice
   that means the `.f` class on every focusable — it is simultaneously the spatial-navigation
   target and the text-to-speech hook.

2. **Spend nothing per frame.** 1 GB and a weak GPU. Move cost to load time, to a fixed
   handful of composited transforms (the two walls, two bars, the trailer card, the face,
   and the five tiles in the row's arc — never all thirty), or out of the product. Pre-rasterise instead of filtering; scale one
   fixed 1920×1080 stage instead of reflowing a responsive layout. Chrome 84 is the floor —
   no `inset`, `aspect-ratio`, `:has()` or `backdrop-filter` — and the build target enforces
   it rather than discipline.

3. **Meet WCAG 2.1 AA, and measure it against the worst case.** 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1
   for non-text and focus, `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured everywhere. On this product the
   worst case is a light sky at its brightest: grade each scrim against the lightest thing
   the room ever shows, not against its average. The per-sky `shade` values and the checks in
   `frontend/scripts/render_sky.py` are how this is held. Ten-foot practice stands alongside
   the standard: nothing below 15 px, focus never invisible and never ambiguous,
   `aria-label`s written as speech (*"Casablanca, Movie, 1942"*) rather than as labels.

4. **A viewer's data belongs to their profile, and a kids profile's limits hold everywhere
   at once** — every row, every search, the resume panel, My List — not only where filtering
   was convenient to apply.

5. **Degrade, never fail; and say what is real.** A missing dataset, unavailable
   `localStorage`, a failed image, an absent Titan SDK, no trailer — each has a defined
   fallback that keeps the screen usable and says something useful. A generated two-tone
   poster card reads as intent; a broken-image icon reads as a bug. Where the product is a
   stub (Watch, Episodes, Continue), it says so instead of pretending.
